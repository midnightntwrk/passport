Section 1

How we got here

From the secure-onboarding six to the working ten

Pass 1 of the planning work started from the six principles in the secure-onboarding document — Key-bound TEE; ZK witnesses, Single seed, three wallets, Chain abstraction via intent, One key per device, Privacy-by-design identity, and Seedless UX. The ten promises above are not a different vision — they are the same six pressure-tested for falsifiability, with mechanism statements peeled off and previously-implicit assumptions made explicit so each can be individually tested. Every promise traces back to something the original document said or assumed; nothing here is invented.

Original principle Disposition New mapping
#1 Key-bound TEE; ZK witnesses Split, abstracted TEE specificity removed; ZK witnesses move to mechanism layer.
#2 Single seed, three wallets Dropped Implementation specification, not a v1.0 invariant.
#3 Chain abstraction via intent Preserved, promoted Same concept, now stated as a v1.0 promise instead of a later-milestone concern.
#4 One key per device Rewritten, split Mechanism statement; surfaces multi-device peerage, no exfiltration, and scoped grants as separate principles.
#5 Privacy-by-design identity Kept, renamed Renamed for sharper testability.
#6 Seedless UX Split No-mnemonic UX is P1; total-loss recovery without seed is P5.

Five made explicit — promises that were implicit in the original document and surfaced as their own principles by Pass 1's pressure-testing.

  • Named— the original mentioned the named-account handle throughout, but never elevated naming to a principle.
  • Peer-device— implicit in #4; multi-device peerage was the premise of the device-add flow.
  • Revoke-and-continue— implicit in the multi-device story; the QR-code device-add flow shadowed a revoke-old-and-continue mode.
  • Scoped grants— function-call keys appeared as a sub-feature of #4 but were never named as their own primitive.
  • Chain-only— assumed throughout the document (no operator was required for any flow), but never stated as a constraint.

Section 6

Parallelisation — streams × sequence

How v1.0 work splits across teams: six streams plus a discovery lane, laid out left-to-right by global dep-depth (longest chain to each component, computed from data.js). Streams are not synchronised — gaps are real, indicating where a stream is idle waiting on something elsewhere. The thick red ribbon traces the longest dependency chain (the critical path); whatever sits on it sets the project's minimum duration. Two pivots are flagged: C1 (the bidirectional core that everything identity-shaped flows through) and C8 (the cross-cutting hash-discipline gate). Below the streams sits the Demo phases lane; click a phase bar to spotlight its components.
