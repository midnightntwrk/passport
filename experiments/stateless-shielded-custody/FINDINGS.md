## Question

Can a contract custody shielded coins without publishing their QSCI in
public ledger state — i.e. is the "QSCI publicity" leak a consequence of
the `insertCoin` storage pattern rather than a ledger inevitability? See
[`EXPERIMENT_GUIDELINE.md`](EXPERIMENT_GUIDELINE.md) for the full framing
and the C4 stakes.

## Hypotheses on the table

1. **H1 (disclosure surface).** The Compact stdlib forces disclosure only of
   hiding hashes (commitment/nullifier links) and the 1-bit change branch —
   never the raw nonce/colour/value. *Evidence: W1 compiler diagnostics.*
2. **H2 (node acceptance).** The node accepts contract shielded spends whose
   QSCI is witness-supplied; the S5 crash was SDK glue, not protocol.
   *Evidence: W3 (escalation: W4).*
3. **H3 (observer surface).** With the QSCI off-ledger, no observer surface
   carries the coin's nonce, colour, or value; the insertCoin control leaks
   all three in contract state. *Evidence: W6.*
4. **H4 (discovery/recovery channel).** The encrypted inbox + indexer
   mt_index capture close the loop for third-party deposits and recovery
   without any off-band channel. *Evidence: W2, W5.*

## Per-probe notes

### W1 — compile-time disclosure

**PASS** (2026/07/03, toolchain 0.31.1). All three undisclosed probe
variants are rejected, and the 41 disclosure clauses across them decompose
cleanly: **33 hiding-hash clauses** (commitment/nullifier "link"
diagnostics — "…the coin with the commitment given by a hash of the witness
value") and **8 single-bit clauses** (booleans of comparisons involving
subtractions on the witness value — the has-change / sufficient-balance
checks inside `sendShielded`/`sendImmediateShielded` — plus the change
conditional). **Zero clauses name a raw coin field as the published
object.** The main contract compiles with the disclosures declared.

Catalogued finding: each stateless spend structurally leaks a handful of
comparison **bits** (change existed or not, balance sufficed) on top of the
hashes. This bounds the leak at bits-per-spend, versus the full
nonce/colour/value per coin under `insertCoin`.

Full verbatim compiler outputs in `evidence/w1-*.compiler.txt`; the
classification in `evidence/w1-compile-disclosure.json`.

### W2 — stateless deposit

**PASS.** The deposit lands with `public_coins` empty and `inbox_count = 1`;
the encrypted inbox blob decrypts back to the exact deposited coin; the
mt_index recovers from the indexer's `startIndex` (single-output
inference). Nothing QSCI-shaped exists in public ledger state.

### W3 — witness spend

**PASS** — the settle-it result. The node **accepts** a contract shielded
spend whose QualifiedShieldedCoinInfo is witness-supplied from wallet-local
storage. The S5 "witness/off-ledger spend is structurally impossible"
reading is refuted empirically on `midnight-node:1.0.0`; contract-held
shielded coins can be spent with no QSCI in public state (phase 1). The
change chain also completes (phase 2): the change is re-owned
in-transaction, returned through the private call result, re-captured with
candidate-mt_index retry (a wrong candidate fails at the prover as an
unsatisfiable witness — prove HTTP 400 — and cannot mis-spend), spent in a
later transaction, and its backup blob appended to the inbox.

**Along the way W3 exposed an upstream bug** (phases 2–3, confirmed by the
insertCoin control comparator failing identically before the fix):
`sendImmediateShielded` re-owns change by **transiently spending it** — the
`result.change` coin's nullifier is burnt in that same transaction and a
NEW coin with an evolved nonce is created, returned as the **`.sent`**
field of its `ShieldedSendResult`. Persisting `result.change` as the held
coin — which OpenZeppelin's `ShieldedTreasury._send` does, and which our
S6 port and the account-custody prototype inherited — leaves an
unspendable zombie: the later spend proves (the commitment is in the tree)
but the node rejects with `Zswap(NullifierAlreadyPresent)` ("attempted
double spend"), surfaced to the SDK as custom error 103 (the node maps the
variant to `ZswapInvalidErrorCode::Unknown`; the true variant is in the
node logs). The one-line fix — persist/return `redeposit.sent` — makes
both the stateless and the insertCoin change chains work. This means the
canonical OZ treasury change path has plausibly never been spent on a real
node (their tests are simulator-only), and the prototype's second
`withdraw_shielded` from the same colour would fail.

### W4 — manual offer (fallback)

Not run — W3 settled the protocol question at the high level, so the
below-SDK path was unnecessary. The runner remains for future stacks.

### W5 — third-party deposit + discovery

**PARTIAL (design works; one indexer gap).** A second wallet deposited
using only public chain data: it read the advertised `enc_key` from
contract state and encrypted the coin info into the deposit blob. The
owner decrypted the inbox, recovered the coin, and spent it
(candidate-mt_index retry, accepted on the first candidate). The single
gap: no probed indexer surface enumerates a contract's transactions by
address (`contractAction(address)` returns a single action), so the owner
fell back to the depositor-known txId for the index window. **C17
finding:** wallet-side discovery of contract-held deposits needs either an
indexer surface (contract address → transactions / Zswap output events) or
a block scan; everything else in the loop needs no off-band channel.

### W6 — observer leak audit

**PASS.** Identical lifecycles through both paths, then a byte-scan of
every observer surface (raw transaction from the indexer, per-call
contract state) for the coin's nonce, colour, and value:

- **insertCoin control:** the coin's **nonce and colour appear verbatim**
  in the raw deposit and spend transactions and in the indexer's per-call
  contract state (the QSCI travels in the call transcript). This is the
  positive control — it validates the scanner and demonstrates the leak
  the pattern carries. (The u128 value did not match the two encodings
  scanned; value-encoding-aware needles are a refinement, but nonce+colour
  publication alone already fully identifies the coin.)
- **Stateless path:** **zero artefacts on every surface** — raw deposit
  tx, raw spend tx, and contract state contain neither the nonce, nor the
  colour, nor the value.

The QSCI-publicity leak is a storage-pattern consequence, not a ledger
inevitability.

## Residual leaks (structural — documented, not probed)

Independent of every probe outcome, two leaks are structural to contract
custody on today's ledger and bound what "private" can mean here:

1. **Contract-address metadata.** Zswap inputs and outputs owned by a
   contract carry the contract address in cleartext
   (`zswap/src/structure.rs`), so deposit/spend **events** for a custody
   contract are countable and timeable by anyone. With one contract per
   account this is a per-user activity graph; a shared custody contract
   with witness-private account binding would widen the anonymity set.
2. **Depositor first-hop traceability.** A contract-owned coin has no owner
   secret, so anyone who knows a coin's info — always including its
   depositor — can compute its nullifier and watch for the spend, and can
   derive same-value churn descendants (nonce evolution is deterministic).
   Breaking the trail requires splitting value or routing through a
   user-owned coin.

## Verdict

**Stateless contract custody of shielded coins is viable on the current
stable stack.** The node accepts witness-supplied QSCI spends including a
full change chain; deposits, discovery, backup, and recovery flow through
an encrypted on-ledger inbox with no off-band channel; and the observer
audit shows zero coin artefacts on the stateless path against a control
that leaks nonce and colour verbatim. All four hypotheses (H1–H4) are
confirmed, H4 with one indexer-surface gap (W5).

Two upstream items fall out:

1. **OZ `ShieldedTreasury` change bug** — `_send` persists
   `result.change` instead of `sendImmediateShielded(...).sent`, leaving
   the treasury's change unspendable on a real node (see W3). Applies to
   every consumer of the pattern, including our account-custody prototype.
2. **Indexer discovery surface** — no query enumerates a contract's
   transactions or Zswap outputs by address, which third-party-deposit
   discovery (and any C17 sync of contract-held coins) needs.

## Implications for the Passport account model

C4 gains a live alternative **A″ — stateless private contract custody**:
the custody and recovery semantics of alternative A (assets follow the
account object; recovery restores the same balances via the encrypted
inbox) with near-user-coin observer privacy. The C4 decision stops being
"accept publicity vs non-custodial"; the residual trade is the structural
metadata documented above (contract-address labelling of deposit/spend
events, depositor first-hop traceability, and bits-per-spend comparison
leakage) plus wallet-side coin-state management (C16 store, C17 sync,
recovery envelope covered by the inbox). The cryptographic-stack design
calibrated to contract custody stands, now without the privacy regression
that motivated reopening the question.
