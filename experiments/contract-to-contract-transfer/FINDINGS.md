## Interpretation

**The technical leg of MIP-0012 6.6 falls; the privacy leg stands and is
now measured.**

1. **The direct transfer works.** One transaction (`007c827b…`) carried
   contract A's `spend_to_contract` (a witness-QSCI spend whose output's
   recipient is contract B's address) and contract B's `deposit_stateless`
   (the `receiveShielded` claim of exactly that coin, plus the encrypted
   inbox blob). The node accepted it; B's inbox grew; A's round advanced;
   the offer was balanced (1 input, 2 outputs, deltas 0). The MIP's
   parenthetical "(Outputs addressed to a different contract are
   additionally rejected by the current ledger)" is true only for a
   single-call transaction: with the claim composed into the same
   transaction, the ledger accepts.

2. **No cross-contract calls were involved.** Composition is client-side,
   through public midnight-js API: `createUnprovenCallTx` per call, then
   grafting B's intent onto A's transaction with
   `Transaction.addIntent({ tag: 'random' }, intentB)` (shielded offers are
   transaction-level; contract calls are intents), then one `submitTx` with
   `circuitId: ['spend_to_contract', 'deposit_stateless']`. Notably,
   `Transaction.merge` is the WRONG tool: B's SDK-built call materialises
   its own copy of the claimed output (the wallet-deposit funding path), so
   a plain merge duplicates the output and the balancer fails with
   `InsufficientFunds` (recorded in the 2026/07/16 intermediate evidence).

3. **The received coin is first-class.** B decrypted the inbox blob,
   recovered the coin's `mt_index` from the indexer candidates, and spent
   it onward to a user key in a later transaction (`0068a01f…`). Direct
   receipt creates no second-class coin.

4. **The privacy cost is exactly as predicted, no worse.** The composed
   transaction exposes both contract addresses together (the accepted
   linking: an observer learns A paid B, or at least that A and B acted in
   one transaction). Value, color, and the coin nonce stayed hidden — the
   byte-scan found neither the 16-byte value encoding, the color, nor the
   nonce anywhere in the indexer-visible surfaces. The stateless custody
   discipline survives direct transfer intact.

5. **Client-side gotchas for any implementer** (worth carrying into
   MIP-0012 implementation notes): the `ContractAddress` circuit argument
   is a struct `{ bytes }`; `deployContract` does not persist
   `initialPrivateState` to the private-state provider; the level
   private-state provider is stateful and scoped per contract address
   (`setContractAddress` before building a call for a second contract);
   `addIntent` requires a `SegmentSpecifier` first argument.

**Consequence for the standard:** the one-hop rule of 6.6 is now motivated
by privacy alone (INV-6 counterparty unlinkability), not by a ledger
limitation. Whether the MUST relaxes to SHOULD (direct transfer permitted
where both parties accept the linking, e.g. payments to a disclosed
merchant account) is a standards decision, queued below.
